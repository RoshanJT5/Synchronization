What Is the Synchronization Android App?

The Synchronization Android app is the receiver half of a two-part wireless audio system. When paired with the Synchronization Chrome Extension running on your desktop, the Android app receives your browser tab audio in real time over a direct Wi-Fi connection — no Bluetooth, no cables, and no cloud server in the middle.

Audio is streamed peer-to-peer using WebRTC technology — the same protocol used for video calls in modern browsers. This means your audio travels the shortest possible path: directly from your PC to your phone over your local network, with no detour through any internet relay.

Once connected, you can walk around your home and continue listening to whatever is playing in Chrome — a movie, a podcast, a music tab, or any other audio source — with virtually zero perceptible delay.

System Requirements

Android Version Android 7.0 (Nougat) or later
Network Wi-Fi or mobile hotspot — same network as your PC
Architecture ARM64 (recommended), ARMv7, or x86_64
Storage Under 20 MB installed
Permissions Camera (QR code scan), Audio playback
Desktop Required Chrome browser with the Synchronization Extension installed

How to Install the App

  1. Download from Google Play — Use the button above to visit the Google Play Store and install the Synchronization app on your Android device.
  2. Launch the Synchronization app — Open the app from your home screen or app drawer.
  3. Pair with the Chrome Extension — On your desktop, click the Synchronization Chrome Extension and press Start Streaming. A QR code appears. In the Android app, tap Scan QR Code and point your camera at the screen. Audio starts within two seconds.
